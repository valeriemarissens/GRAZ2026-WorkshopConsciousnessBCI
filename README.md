## Abstract
Reliable assessment of awareness from brain activity remains a major challenge in
neuroscience and clinical practice. In situations such as general anesthesia or disorders of
consciousness, behavioral responses may be absent or unreliable, making objective
consciousness monitoring tools critically needed. Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs), combined
with EEG-based approaches, provide promising methods to detect covert cognitive processing
and monitor changes in brain states across a continuum of consciousness conditions. This
workshop will present recent advances in EEG and BCI methods aimed at assessing and
tracking consciousness across different clinical and experimental contexts. Contributions will
cover somatosensory stimulation-based BCIs, neural criticality metrics, covert cognition
detection, and neuro-prognostication in coma patients approaches. By bringing together experts
working across anesthesia and disorders of consciousness, this workshop aims to identify
shared methodological challenges and promising directions for reliable clinical translation. Short
presentations combined with structured interactive discussions will foster interdisciplinary
exchanges between BCI researchers, neuroscientists, and clinicians.

Talks (45 min):**
- “Detecting Accidental Awareness during General Anesthesia with Neuromuscular Blockade: Challenges and Considerations” - Sebastian Halder (15 min)
- “Towards a BCI-based marker of conscious processing during anesthesia” - Valérie Marissens Cueva (15 min)

**Interactive discussions with the audience** (animated by Laurent Bougrain) (30 min)
This discussion will focus on identifying key methodological and clinical challenges in monitoring
consciousness during general anesthesia. Participants will be encouraged to share their
perspectives based on both experimental and clinical experience.
- What EEG or BCI markers provide the most reliable indicators of awareness during
anesthesia?
- Which paradigms are the most robust in real surgical environments?
- How should the “ground truth” be defined when assessing intraoperative awareness?
- What level of sensitivity and specificity is clinically required?
- What methodological, practical and data-related challenges still limit clinical deployment?

**Interactive discussions with the audience** (animated by Fabien Lotte) (30 min)
This session will address the challenges of detecting residual or covert cognition in patients with
disorders of consciousness and coma, with a particular focus on translational and clinical
implications.
- How can covert cognition be reliably detected in non-responsive patients?
- Which paradigms and biomarkers are most robust across patients and conditions?
- Are the biomarkers relevant for general anesthesia also relevant for coma and DOC?

- How can variability across patients be addressed?
- What ethical challenges arise when detecting covert cognition?
